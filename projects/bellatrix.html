Operation Bellatrix — COMCYBER CTF · ORION 26
Operation Bellatrix (COMCYBER + CIAE, ORION 26, 23–27 March 2026) was a national CTF built around a fictional influence campaign and the search for a downed pilot. The event mixed disinformation analysis, technical attribution, and OSINT / GEOINT across three days.

01. day 1 — analysis / disinformation

Bienvenue

Open briefing on the platform. Framed the fictional influence case and the rules of engagement for the investigation.

Un message d'alerte

Open web / screenshot OSINT. Started from a thin social alert that pointed to a disinfo blog, then found that blog on the public internet despite incomplete clues and decoys.

La vidéo du sauvetage

Open web + HTML/source check. Tracked a “pilot rescue” video from the disinfo blog back to the hosting site that first published the clip, instead of trusting the blog’s framing alone.

Trop beau pour être vrai

Brand / logo OSINT. Checked whether the fake news site was impersonating a real outlet (lookalike logo / naming) to borrow credibility.

Au service d'une cause

Narrative reuse. Followed how the same rescue video was copied onto another activist-style site and how the wording pushed an anti-army story.

Rétablir la véracité des faits

Official debunk trail. Cross-checked the viral claim against public fact-check / “official debunk” channels that contradicted the disinfo narrative and named the pilot callsign in the scenario.

Identifier le primo-diffuseur

SOCMINT. Worked backwards through comments and social posts to find who seeded the story first on the fictional social network before the blogs amplified it.

Une metadata vaut mille images

Image forensics. Pulled technical metadata from a suspicious “photo” used in the campaign to see whether it looked generated / edited rather than a real field shot.

(Dé)-Crédibiliser l'information

Fake-expert check. Verified cited “experts” and credentials in the articles against open sources to spot invented authority used to sell the lie.

Cyber-physique !

DISARM / influence TTPs. Mapped a late-day call-to-action post onto a public disinformation framework (theme: how online speech tries to spill into real-world pressure).

Analyser l'attaque

CSV / social dump analysis. Looked at scraped social activity for coordinated inauthentic behaviour — repeated hashtags and swarming accounts amplifying the same narrative.

Mesurer l'attaque

Temporal metrics. Measured how fast those hashtag waves went from first post to peak volume, to describe the pacing of the info-ops burst.

02. day 2 — technical attribution

Attribution technique

Infra pivot. Moved from narrative clues to technical breadcrumbs that could tie sites and accounts together.

Surveillance du réseau social

SOCMINT monitoring. Watched the suspect social profiles for reusable links, habits, and cross-posts into the wider campaign.

La piste de l'organisation

Org mapping on open pages. Followed public “about” / showcase pages toward a structure (people + front businesses) behind the influence push.

Fouiller le site

Web recon. Browsed suspect sites methodically (menus, about, leftovers) for developer names and operational hints left in plain sight.

Nos réalisations …

Portfolio / showcase mining. Used a public “our work” style page to list more domains tied to the same builder trail.

La piste du financement

Follow-the-money (public). Looked at the façade shop / payment-adjacent trail for open clues linking money flow to the influence actors.

Analyse WHOIS des domaines suspects

Open WHOIS. Queried registration data on the suspect domains to spot shared owner / registrar / creation patterns.

Découverte de preuves cachées

Hidden paths. Used common public files (robots, sitemaps, leftover folders) to surface less-obvious material still reachable from the web.

Les Wordpress d'Hélène Volquoffe

CMS fingerprinting. Correlated several WordPress sites attributed to the same developer and looked for a recurring technical signature across them.

La temporalité de l'attaque

Timeline. Ordered public posts and site events on one clock to see how the technical and narrative pieces lined up.

Réutilisations de code sur les domaines

Code / infra reuse. Compared HTML/JS/DNS-style fingerprints across domains to show they were cut from the same cloth.

Déclenchement immédiat

Access / trigger puzzle (theme level). Worked a final technical gate on the campaign infra (credential / unlock style challenge) that “started” the next narrative beat — no credential or flag published here.

03. day 3 — OSINT & GEOINT

OSINT

Open-source framing. Shifted fully to people, places, and public records to close the search for the fictional pilot.

En chemin pour une partie de pêche

Travel / review OSINT. Used open reviews and check-ins along a coastal-to-inland trail left by a key persona during the disappearance window.

Remonter la piste

Link analysis. Chained persons, companies, sites and places into one investigation graph (the sketch later shown on this page).

Les archives au secours

Web archives. Recovered older public copies when live pages were thin or cleaned.

Le plein trop cher

Local economic OSINT. Used a mundane fuel / price-style clue as a geographic pivot toward the right area.

Localisation de "Lynx"

GEOINT (main plot). Combined open imagery, land / parcel style clues and field photos to pin the fictional holding location — coordinates and how-to withheld.

On se rapproche

GEOINT refine. Narrowed the search box with extra open geo signals once the area of interest was known.

Temps incertains

Temporal OSINT. Reconciled conflicting public timestamps around the endgame so the story timeline still held together.
